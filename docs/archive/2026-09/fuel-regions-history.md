# Fuel planning beyond delivery

## Manual rolling horizon (September 6 update)

This section supersedes the older automatic/full-tank behavior described below.

- Fuel search runs only through **Find fuel stops / Recalculate fuel**. Background tracking prepares routes, but never replaces purchase recommendations.
- IFTA eligibility and economic prices use the global Settings preference, independently of the map's display toggle.
- The search starts at fresh GPS, skips completed current stops, and appends up to two subsequent assigned trips, including connecting deadhead. It stops adding trips after 1,200 miles or 48 driving hours; future trips starting beyond 72 hours or taking the complete horizon beyond 1,800 miles are not added. The current trip is never truncated. This is not a full calendar/HOS scheduling solver.
- Route variants keep identical mandatory endpoints. Fuel recommendations are stored separately: current dispatch distance, route geometry and route version are not replaced by future-trip geometry. Leg boundaries preserve stop order even when later trips revisit earlier locations.
- The terminal policy keeps reserve plus checked access to fuel. In expensive or poorly served destination regions it requires at least 50% of physical tank capacity, or the checked exit requirement if larger. This is a hard minimum, not a speculative credit. Purchases are optimized against that common minimum: fill early when cheaper and top up later only when needed and cost-effective, respecting working fill limits. Truck parking availability, yard choices, appointment/HOS feasibility and toll differences are not verified by this version. Recommendations are not automatically dispatched to drivers.
- Starting fuel uses the latest available valid percentage even if its timestamp is old or missing. This is an estimate based on that reading, not a claim of live tank accuracy. Missing or invalid percentages still prevent calculation. Reading age alone does not hide an otherwise valid recommendation.
- Changed settings, assignments, route, stale GPS, material fuel deviation or a plan older than 30 minutes invalidate recommendations. The age check is a conservative price-review trigger, not real-time price change detection.
- Starting a manual recalculation clears the previous saved recommendations. Failure leaves no suggested stations, with no user-facing error or success notification. Ordinary station catalogue markers remain controlled by the Fuel Stations toggle. Client failure handling also removes cached recommendations.
- Tests cover manual-only background behavior, Settings-only IFTA changes, two subsequent trips with deadhead, unchanged current route distance, and persistent removal of failed recommendations.

The optimizer now has a post-delivery inventory policy rather than treating delivery as the end of fuel demand. It first checks an exit to a priced station using truck routing. The minimum arrival inventory is the separate safety reserve plus fuel for that exit (or the configured minimum post-delivery driving buffer, whichever is larger). If this is infeasible, it reports an unavailable plan instead of silently reducing the reserve.

An approximately square projected grid (50 miles at latitude 40 by default) groups the known diesel network. Cells contain a station count and median normalized USD/US-gallon price. Empty cells mean **no known priced stations**, not proof that no stations exist. `good`, `expensive`, `sparse`, and `unknown` describe fuel coverage/prices, not neighborhood safety. IFTA, date, currency, unit, and CAD exchange-rate eligibility are consistent for route and destination comparisons.

The delivery cell and surrounding stations determine whether a larger working fuel buffer is desirable. When a next pickup is assigned, the exit candidates are limited to its route corridor or pickup vicinity. Otherwise the planner examines the area around delivery without inventing a subsequent load. This protects access to fuel after delivery; it does not optimize all remaining stops of future dispatches.

When delivery has poor fuel coverage/prices and no next dispatch is assigned, the final planned purchase fills to the configured maximum. This rule can add a top-up even when delivery is already reachable. A tank above 80% does not force another stop. If no verified station is reachable, the planner retains a feasible reserve-only plan rather than inventing a purchase.

Before a poor-fuel destination, the optimizer also checks the last verified station priced at least `ExpensivePremiumUsdPerGallon` below the cheapest eligible local destination price. If predicted arrival there is at or below 80% of tank capacity, it tops up to the configured fill limit, even after filling at an earlier cheaper station. Above 80%, this extra stop is not required. Road detours, reserve and ability to reach delivery remain constraints; the rule uses cached station prices and adds no routing calls.

Planned purchases must be at least 10 US gallons. A station with more than 80% fuel on arrival is skipped when delivery plus the required exit reserve is already reachable. A token purchase cannot bypass the final maximum-fill rule. Station popups show purchase volume and distance without post-delivery explanatory paragraphs.

In poor fuel areas, the optional inventory target is at least the configured fill limit and is valued at the checked exit station's price. This values the useful fuel remaining in each variant consistently; it does not require arriving with a full tank. In ordinary areas the configured driving buffer remains the target. Thus buying more now can beat buying expensive fuel later, even if current fuel already reaches delivery. Stop costs still discourage tiny savings. Physical detour fuel, capacity, configured fill percentage, and reserve are always constraints. The separate under-five-mile detour ranking preference remains in place.

`FuelRegions` in API appsettings controls cell size, expensive-price premium, minimum known station count, search radii, arrival driving buffers, and maximum road checks. Defaults: 75-mile minimum arrival buffer, 200-mile optional buffer for poor fuel coverage/prices, and at most three exit station checks per plan. An assigned next pickup may require one additional route and a geocode if its coordinates are missing. Existing TomTom request limits and 12-hour cache apply. Grid calculations use cached fuel prices, not a request per square or GPS update. The existing candidate shortlist is a bounded heuristic, not an exhaustive global optimum.

Each stored fuel plan includes the policy, grid cells, checked exit, predicted arrival gallons, and algorithm/configuration version. There is no timer-based fuel recalculation: normal driving, passing a fuel station, changed tank readings, old GPS, and date changes keep the saved plan. New routes, changed vehicle/stops, and explicit planning/configuration changes invalidate it. Automatic rerouting requires a deviation over `Synchronization.RouteDeviationMiles` (5 miles) for `RouteDeviationSeconds` (120 seconds), with the existing five-minute reroute cooldown. Routing back to a still-pending dispatch stop is also preserved. Future dispatch assignments and new prices are considered on the next actual recalculation. No database migration is needed because plans are stored as JSON.

`Recalculate fuel` on the map calls `POST /api/dispatch/{id}/planning/fuel/recalculate`. It uses the saved truck profile, latest available fuel reading, and current prices, sharing the background planner's gate. It compares complete truck routes through shortlisted stations and preserves the previous route and fuel plan if calculation fails. Successful selection saves fuel quantities and route geometry in one update. Refreshing the page only reads the plan; GPS updates continue to update distances and hide passed station highlights without making new route-provider calls.

The regional grid is used internally by the fuel planner; it is not shown as a map overlay. Only actual planned purchases are highlighted. The ordinary map IFTA toggle affects price colors; regional planning uses the IFTA preference from Settings.


Alternative station search uses `FuelRegions.CandidateSearchMiles` (30), `CandidateRoadChecks` (12), and `MaximumExtraMiles` (40). Each variant runs from the current position through candidate stations and every pending mandatory dispatch stop, in order. No return-to-original-road anchor is imposed. The shortlist covers sections across the entire route and prioritizes reachable stations and the last affordable stations before delivery. `CandidateShortlistLimit` (24) controls local candidates separately from paid road checks. Before calling the routing provider, local optimization ranks multi-station seeds, substitutions, extra top-ups, individual stations and pairs using purchase, stop and terminal fuel costs. Only the best-ranked variants within the road-check budget are verified. It is not an exhaustive search. Total additional miles and time are compared with a fresh baseline through the same mandatory stops; `MaxDetourMinutes` still applies. Differences below five miles omit the time-ranking penalty, but actual fuel consumption is always counted.

The winning route is collapsed to one leg per mandatory stop, retaining fuel waypoints inside the geometry. Route version, total distance, and fuel plan are stored together; concurrent changes reject the result. SelectionVersion 9 identifies the new selection logic. Cached routing responses and provider quotas still apply; opening the page does not run this search.

The popup uses `FillToTarget` to display **Fill up** without quantities. The number is the amount to add, not the total fuel after the stop. Non-full purchases retain the `Buy` label.

Hybrid geographic search groups candidate stations into the existing projected cells. Median normalized prices rank groups; real station coordinates, never cell centers, are used as routing waypoints. Inexpensive groups may contribute stations up to `ZoneSearchMiles` (60) from the reference route, in addition to the regular 30-mile corridor. `ZoneRoadChecks` (3) reserves slots inside the existing 12-route budget for representative station routes even if the projected quantity optimizer recommends no purchase. Baseline and two best preliminary chains precede these checks. The remaining budget checks the other economic scenarios. Several finalist chains are expanded with earlier fills/later top-ups, including three-stop alternatives to a two-stop fallback.

`FuelPlan.RouteChecks` stores the checked station names, additional mileage/time, comparison cost when available, and the reason for rejection or selection. It is diagnostic data and is not added to station popup text. A low-priced region does not override reserve or truck-road/detour constraints. Candidate and paid-check limits mean the search remains a bounded heuristic.
